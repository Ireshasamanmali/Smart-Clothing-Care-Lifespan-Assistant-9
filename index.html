Microbial Thresholds

How Mould & Mildew Attack Fabrics

Mildew refers to pigmented fungal hyphae growing on organic surfaces. Optimal growth occurs between 20°C and 30°C (68°–86°F) when relative humidity is 70% or higher in dark, poorly ventilated cupboards.

  • Visual signature: White powder, irregular blotches, or a symmetric cross/star pattern spreading along both warp and fill threads.
  • Temperature truth: Freezing below 0°C does not kill mildew spores; only temperatures above 46°C (115°F), UV sunlight, or dry air arrest growth.
  • Bacteria vs Fungi: Water-damaged textiles contaminated by bacteria emit a sour odour first before turning musty.
Fibre Susceptibility

Cellulose vs Protein vs Synthetic

  • Highest Risk (Cellulose & Leather): Cotton, linen, ramie, rayon, leather, suede, and natural rubber provide abundant nutrients. Mildew engrains into leather pores, causing thin spots and permanent dye stains.
  • Moderate Resistance: Polyester, nylon, acetate, and acrylic resist mildew but still stain if soiled with body oils or unsanitary dampness.
  • Highest Natural Resistance: Silk and wool resist mildew unless left damp for prolonged periods.
Remediation Chemistry

Bleach & Odour Removal Safety

  • Ozone before washing: Dry items treated with ozone permanently oxidise musty odours before stain removal (avoid rubber and acetate dyes).
  • Chlorine bleach: Only bleach that kills mildew, safe on white cotton/linen/ramie after colourfastness testing; never use on silk, wool, nylon, or coloured fabrics.
  • Gentle alternatives: Sodium perborate (safe on most wool/silk/nylon, watch optical brighteners) or 3% hydrogen peroxide (mildest, safe on most colours).
Tropical Chemistry

Why Colours Fade Faster in Humid Climates

In high-humidity regions, ambient moisture interacts with dye molecules through hydrolysis, weakening their bond to the fibre. Combined with hot water and drum friction, knit fabrics lose surface saturation in as few as 15–40 cycles.

Structure Matters

Knits vs Woven Construction

Knit fabrics (T-shirts, sportswear jerseys) consist of interlocking loops that rub against one another in the drum, creating micro-abrasions that scatter light and make black garments look charcoal. Tightly woven shirts and trousers resist this mechanical abrasion better.

4-Step Retention Protocol

Cold, Inside-Out & Liquid Care

  • Turn inside out: Shields outer dyed yarns from drum friction.
  • Cold water (≤30°C): Prevents fibre swelling and dye release while lifting sweat salts.
  • Liquid over powder: Powder residues stiffen fabric in humid weather and trap moisture.
  • Air dry in shade: Direct tropical UV bleaches dyes rapidly; dry in ventilated shade.
